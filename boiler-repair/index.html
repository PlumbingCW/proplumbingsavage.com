A boiler circulates hot water through radiators, baseboards or radiant floor loops. When it stops, a Minnesota winter finds out fast. We diagnose and repair boilers in Savage, from no-heat calls to leaks, noisy pumps and failing relief valves.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first. If there is no heat but no smell, check the thermostat, the emergency switch near the boiler and the breaker.

Boilers last many years when maintained. An annual service catches worn parts before the first cold night.

Repair or replace.

An old cast iron boiler can run for decades. A newer high-efficiency unit may have complex parts and board failures. We help you weigh what a repair buys compared with a swap.

Savage and boiler heat.

Homes with hydronic heat in Savage are often older houses and some custom builds with radiant floors. Water quality matters. Hard water, reported in the Burnsville supply at 25 to 26 grains per gallon, can scale a boiler's heat exchanger if it is fed fresh water regularly through leaks. A leak that keeps topping up the system is worth fixing for this reason alone.

Minnesota winters test every heating system. If you rely on a boiler, schedule service in early fall rather than during the first cold snap.

Before you call: five quick checks.

  • Is the thermostat set to heat and calling above room temperature?
  • Is the switch on the boiler or on the wall near the stairs on?
  • Does the pressure gauge read in the normal range printed on the boiler?
  • Is the breaker for the boiler and circulator pump on?
  • Are the radiator valves and zone valves open?

If all five check out and there is still no heat, note any error code on the display and the sound the boiler makes when it tries to start. Clicking without ignition points to one set of causes. A humming pump with no flame points to another. That information helps us arrive with the right parts.

Never bleed radiators on a hot system, and never open a relief valve to test it unless the maker's manual says so. Both can scald.

If your boiler is fed by oil or propane rather than natural gas, tell us when you call, because the fuel changes the service.
