A leak you cannot see is still a leak. We find hidden leaks in Savage homes, and we answer 24/7 when water is already damaging walls or ceilings. Shut off the main water valve if you see active water, keep power away from wet areas and call us for the same-day visit when schedules allow.

Not every leak is dramatic. A toilet that runs on, a stain that grows slowly or a bill that keeps climbing can each point to a pipe that is losing water somewhere out of sight.

When a hidden leak is an emergency.

Water through a ceiling, a wall that is wet to the touch or a floor that feels warm where it should not are urgent signs. So is a basement that suddenly smells of damp. Act quickly, because wet drywall and subfloor can grow mold in days.

  • Water dripping from a light fixture or ceiling: turn off power to that area at the breaker first.
  • A section of floor that is warm or soft.
  • The sound of running water when every fixture is off.
  • A water meter that spins when nothing is in use.
  • A sudden drop in water pressure.

The meter test: start with a number.

Many leak calls start with a high bill. Turn off every fixture, find the meter and watch the small dial or flow indicator. If it moves, water is going somewhere. Then close the main valve inside the house and look again. If the meter stops, the leak is in the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is in the line between the meter and the house.

Savage homes get a utility bill from the City of Savage. A reading that jumps without a change in habits is worth chasing early. We combine that figure with a physical search so we know where to look.

Savage conditions to consider.

Savage sits beside the Minnesota River, with the Credit River and Eagle Creek crossing the city. High groundwater in lower areas can make a damp basement look like a plumbing leak. We tell the two apart, and if it is groundwater we point you to sump pump repair or drainage fixes rather than chasing a pipe that is not leaking.

Winters here freeze the ground and can crack pipes in uninsulated walls and crawl spaces. A frozen line that splits may not show itself until it thaws, so a spring bill spike is a common clue.

When the water meter keeps moving.

A meter that turns while every fixture is off is one of the clearest signs of a hidden leak. A higher water bill with no change in habits points the same way. You can check it yourself. Turn everything off, read the meter, wait an hour without using water and read it again. Any change means water is going somewhere.

The next question is where. The leak may be inside the house, under a slab or on the pipe between the street and your home. We isolate each section until the meter stops. If the loss is outside the house, our water service line repair page explains what comes next.

Water stains on a ceiling or wall.

A stain on a ceiling or wall tells you where water ended up, not where it started. Water runs along framing and pipes before it shows. A stain under a bathroom is often a supply line, a drain or a shower pan, and each needs a different fix.

Turn off the water to the room above if you can, and keep people clear of a bulging ceiling. Do not poke it unless you have a bucket ready. We trace the source with moisture readings and then open the smallest area needed, so the repair stays small.

Slab leaks under the floor.

Homes with a concrete slab hide their pipes under the floor. Warm spots on the floor, the sound of running water when nothing is on and damp flooring are the usual signs. A hot water line under the slab often shows as a warm patch and a hot water bill that climbs.

Once the line is located, the options are a spot repair, a reroute through the walls or, if the pipe is failing in many places, repiping. We explain which one fits the pipe's condition before work starts.
