A sewer camera inspection puts a waterproof lens on a flexible cable and feeds it through the line from the cleanout toward the street. The video shows the pipe wall, the joints and anything inside. It is the fastest way to turn a guess into a diagnosis.

We do planned inspections for buyers, for homeowners with repeat clogs and for anyone about to pay for a repair they want confirmed first. If sewage is already backing up, our urgent sewer pages are the better starting point.

Who benefits from an inspection.

  • Home buyers who want to know what is under the lawn before closing.
  • Owners whose drain clogs every few months.
  • Anyone who got a sewer repair quote and wants a second look.
  • Owners of older homes with clay, cast iron or orangeburg pipe.
  • Properties with big trees near the line.

Buying a house in Savage's older core, including the Hamilton District near Highway 13, is a good time to scope. The lateral may be decades old, and a camera costs far less than a surprise repair.

What Savage buyers and owners should know.

The City of Savage owns the water and sewer mains, and the homeowner usually owns the lateral that connects the house. Ask City of Savage Utility Services where the dividing line sits for your address. That tells you what your camera footage is worth when you negotiate a repair.

Minnesota's Gopher State One Call service, reached by dialing 811, is free and requires at least 48 hours notice before digging, not counting weekends and holidays. We raise it whenever an inspection leads to excavation.

Reading the footage with us.

Camera footage looks alarming to anyone who has not seen it before. Roots look like hair, scale looks like rust and a normal joint looks like a seam. We narrate the video, pause on each finding and tell you whether it is cosmetic, worth watching or worth fixing. You leave knowing which three findings matter and which ten do not.

If you live in the Hamilton District and are thinking about an inspection, that page lists what the city has published about the area and how to find out what your own water line is made of.
