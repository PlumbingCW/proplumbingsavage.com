Repiping in Savage, MN

Whole-house and partial supply line replacement for failing galvanized, polybutylene or pinhole-leaking copper.

Repiping replaces the supply lines that carry water through a home. It is a planned project for pipes that keep failing, not an emergency fix. If a pipe has burst, see our emergency burst pipe page first and come back here once the water is off.

Signs your pipes may be due.

  • Repeated pinhole leaks in copper.
  • Brown or rusty water from the tap, especially in the morning.
  • Low pressure that gets worse with time.
  • Galvanized steel pipe, which corrodes from the inside.
  • Polybutylene pipe, a grey plastic used in some older homes and known for failures.

One leak is a repair. Three leaks in a year in different places is a pattern, and chasing them with patches costs more than a plan.

Pipe materials and what to expect.

What Savage homeowners should check.

The City of Savage's recent lead and copper sampling found 0 of 32 homes above the action level, with next testing due in 2027. That tells you about the city's system at the tap, not about your plumbing. If your home has old lead-soldered copper, galvanized pipe or an unknown service line, a repipe is the time to deal with it.

Savage's supply is also hard, drawn largely from Burnsville at a reported 25 to 26 grains per gallon, and mineral buildup speeds corrosion in some pipes. A water softener plan can be part of a repipe project.

What to ask before you sign a repipe contract.

  • Which pipe material is quoted, and why that one for this house?
  • How many access openings will be cut, and where?
  • How long will water be off each day?
  • Who closes the walls and paints?
  • Is the main shutoff valve replaced as part of the work?
  • What does the warranty cover on pipe, fittings and labor?

We answer these in writing. A repipe is a once-in-decades job, and a clear scope up front prevents the unpleasant surprises that make people regret it.

Staying in the house during the work.

Most households stay home during a repipe. We set up so one bathroom and the kitchen sink have water for most of each day, and we tell you in advance when the supply is off. Keep a few jugs of drinking water and plan showers around the schedule. If someone in the home needs steady water for medical reasons, tell us at the survey so we plan the sequence around it.
