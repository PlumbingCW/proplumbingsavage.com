A tankless unit heats water as it flows, so you do not run out in the middle of a shower. In Savage it works best when it is sized to the home and maintained against hard-water scale. We install, flush and repair tankless units, and we say plainly when a tank is the better fit.

Is tankless right for this house?

Tankless saves floor space and can last longer than a tank when it is flushed on schedule. It also needs a bigger gas line, a proper vent and, for electric models, a heavy electrical supply. Many older homes need more work to make that happen.

Hard water and tankless units.

Savage buys most of its water from Burnsville, and Burnsville reports hardness of 25 to 26 grains per gallon. Scale forms fastest inside the narrow passages of a tankless heat exchanger. A unit in hard water that never gets flushed can lose output, throw error codes or fail long before its time.

We service this with a descaling flush using a pump and a descaling solution, then check the inlet filter and water flow. In homes with hard supply, a water softener before the heater is the more lasting answer.

Repairs and flush service.

Error codes, a unit that cuts out between showers, weak hot flow and a cold sandwich effect (hot, cold, hot) all have causes we can trace. Often it is scale, a clogged inlet screen or a venting problem. We read the code, test the flow and explain what we found.

If your unit is a few years old and has never been flushed, call sooner than later. Heavy scale can need a longer cleaning and sometimes a part.

Gas line and venting: the part most quotes gloss over.

A gas tankless unit burns far more gas per minute than a tank burner, even though it runs for less time. If the existing gas pipe is sized for a tank, the unit may starve at peak flow and shut itself off. We measure the run from the meter and check the regulator before we promise anything.

Venting is the other surprise. Condensing units produce acidic condensate that needs a drain and approved vent material. Older chimney venting rarely works. We walk the vent route with you during the site check so the quote includes it.
