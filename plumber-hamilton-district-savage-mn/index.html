Where the Hamilton District sits.

The City of Savage's 2040 Comprehensive Plan places the Hamilton District in the northeastern part of town, south of the port and its industry. It runs along Highway 13 between the Savage and Burnsville boundary and the Canadian Pacific Railroad. This page is for homes and small buildings in that stretch.

The plan calls it a residential area built in the 1930s and 1940s, with a traditional layout of sidewalks, alleys and on-street parking. It also notes that the Savage Depot was brought back to the district as a gathering place.

What the city replaced, and what it did not.

The same plan says nearly all of the area's streets and utilities were obsolete and in need of replacement, and that a multi-phase infrastructure program has since been completed. That describes the public side, meaning the streets and the mains under them.

It does not describe the pipes inside a home or the line that runs from the street to the house. Those depend on each building, and the age of a neighborhood does not tell us what is inside a wall. We look first and then tell you what we find.

Questions that come up in an older core.

  • A drain that backs up more than once. A sewer camera inspection shows the line instead of guessing, and then sewer line cleaning or repair follows if the picture calls for it.
  • A slow kitchen or bathroom drain. Start with drain cleaning and tell us if it keeps returning.
  • A water bill that jumps with no change in use. Leak detection starts with a meter check before any wall is opened.
  • A buyer or owner who wants the water line checked before a remodel. See water service line repair.

The water line and the lead inventory.

The City of Savage lead service line page lists its citywide inventory as zero known lead lines, 2 galvanized lines, 1,065 of unknown material and 9,058 non-lead lines. Those counts are for the whole city, not for this district.

To learn what your own line is made of, call City of Savage Utility Services at 952-224-3440 or look up your address in the state's Lead Inventory Tracking Tool. Ask the city who owns which part of the line before you pay for any work.

Before you dig or remodel.

Minnesota law requires a call to Gopher State One Call at 811 before digging. That includes a trench for a new water or sewer line. Make the call a few days ahead so the utilities can mark their lines.
