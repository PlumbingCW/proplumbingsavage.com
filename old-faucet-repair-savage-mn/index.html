Old Faucet Repair in Savage, MN

What wears out, whether parts exist and a simple way to decide between a repair and a new faucet.

By the Savage Pro Plumbing team. Published and updated 2026-11-10.

Talk to a plumber(952) 866-9120Call Now

An old faucet can last decades, and many are better built than new ones. They also develop leaks, stiff handles and weak flow. Whether to repair or replace depends on parts, finish and your plans. This guide helps you decide.

What wears out on an old faucet.

  • Rubber washers and seats in compression faucets.
  • O-rings in single-handle faucets.
  • Cartridges, which stiffen with scale.
  • Aerators clogged by mineral buildup.
  • Supply lines and shutoff valves under the sink.

Most of these are inexpensive to replace. The question is whether the right part exists.

Can you still get parts?

Major brands support their faucets for many years. Small or discontinued brands may not. A plumber can often match a cartridge from a photo or measurements. If you cannot identify the brand, a universal repair kit may not fit.

The shutoff valves matter more than the faucet.

Under the sink, the small stop valves feed the faucet. Old ones seize and leak. If they do not close, you cannot work on the faucet without shutting the whole house off. Replacing them during a faucet job is cheap insurance.

Hard water and aging fixtures.

Scale clogs aerators and wears seals. Soaking an aerator in vinegar often restores flow. A cartridge that has stiffened may only need cleaning. If you find yourself descaling often, a softener solves the root cause.

How to identify an old faucet without a label.

Look at the handle shape and mounting. Look under the sink for a model stamp on the underside of the faucet body. Check the supply line threads and the number of holes in the sink. Measure the distance between handle centers on a two-handle faucet. These details narrow a match even for brands that no longer exist.

Take the old cartridge or stem to a plumbing supply counter. Clerks can often match a part by sight.

Stop when something does not move. A seized part is a sign to call, because forcing it cracks the body. Replacing a faucet costs more than a repair, but a broken one costs far more.

Vintage and specialty faucets.

Some older faucets are worth saving. Heavy brass bodies, porcelain-lever handles and classic wall-mounted designs may look better and last longer than their modern replacements. Reproduction cartridges and washers exist for many of them. If you like the look, tell us. We can often source parts from specialty suppliers or recondition the valve.

If a faucet is a family piece or fits a period house, the repair cost may be worth it even if a new faucet would be cheaper.

Costs to keep in mind.

A repair is usually small. A replacement adds the price of the faucet, a new set of supply lines and any parts needed to fit the sink holes. Compare the total to the repair with one extra question: how long do you expect to keep the sink and countertop? If a kitchen remodel is two years away, repair now and replace later.

One more practical point: take a photo of the faucet and the shutoff valves before you call anyone. A clear picture saves a wasted trip and speeds the quote.

What to know in Savage.

Savage buys about 71.5 percent of its distributed water from Burnsville, and Burnsville's 2025 report states hardness of 25 to 26 grains per gallon. Savage's own report does not list hardness, so test your tap. Hard water is why faucets in this area scale up quickly.

The Savage Depot, the Hamilton District and the older blocks near Highway 13 have homes with fixtures from several eras. When a house has been remodeled in stages, you may find faucets of different ages in one home.
