Interior drainage systems.

An interior system is a channel at the base of the wall that collects water under the slab edge and carries it to a sump pit. The pump discharges it away from the house. It does not stop water from entering the wall. It gives the water a path so it does not reach the floor.

Exterior vs interior.

Exterior waterproofing digs down the outside of the foundation to seal and drain it. It addresses the wall directly but costs far more, disturbs landscaping and often needs more access. Interior systems are the more common choice for retrofits, and exterior work fits when a wall itself has failed.

Savage homes near the Credit River, Eagle Creek and the Savage Fen see seasonal high groundwater. A basement on higher ground may only need good gutters and grading. A basement in a low area often benefits from interior drainage with a pump and backup.

Things you can do this weekend.

Before paying for a system, rule out the free causes. Walk the outside of the house during a rainstorm and watch where the water goes. Downspouts should deliver water at least several feet from the foundation. Soil should slope away from the walls. Window wells should drain, and covers keep leaves out.

Inside, check the laundry hose, the water heater, the humidifier and any exposed pipes. A drip tray under a washer that overflows looks the same as seepage. Use a flashlight along the base of each wall at night, when shadows reveal damp patches.

Write down the date, the weather and which wall was wet. That record helps us choose a fix, and it helps an insurer if you make a claim.
