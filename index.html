Emergency plumber in Savage, available 24/7.

A plumbing emergency does not keep business hours. A pipe splits at two in the morning, a toilet overflows on a holiday or a basement drain bubbles during a storm. We answer the 24/7 line, ask a few questions and give you the first steps while a plumber heads your way.

The first steps are simple. Turn off the main water valve if water is loose. Stop using drains if sewage is rising. Keep people away from wet electrical areas. These actions often limit the damage more than anything that happens next.

Before the emergency plumber arrives.

  1. Shut off the main water valve.

    Turn it clockwise until it stops. If you cannot find it, call and we help over the phone.

    .

  2. Stop using drains.

    Do not flush or run the washer if a drain is backing up.

What Savage water does to plumbing.

The City of Savage buys about 71.5 percent of its distributed water from Burnsville and supplements it with its own wells. Burnsville's 2025 report states hardness of 25 to 26 grains per gallon. Savage's own report does not list hardness, so test your tap or ask City of Savage Utility Services.

Burnsville (reported)25 to 26 gpg
Prior Lake (city page)19.3 gpg
Lakeville (city page)18 gpg

Figures come from each city's own water report or page. Savage and Shakopee do not state a hardness figure in their reports, so test at your tap.

Hard water scales water heaters, clogs aerators and narrows pipes. It is why so many local calls end in a flush, a part or a softener.

A note on Savage water.

The City of Savage has posted an update on radium in its water. After a 2023 test showed radium above the limit at Water Treatment Plant 3, the City took that plant offline and states the situation is not an emergency. The City's page says water softening, reverse osmosis and distillation may help reduce radium, and carbon filters are not recommended.

Questions about your water belong with City of Savage Utility Services and your doctor. If you want equipment advice, our water softener and filtration team can talk through the options.

Savage places we know.

The Hamilton District and downtown Savage along Highway 13 are the oldest part of town. Savage was a river landing in the 1850s, took the name Savage in 1904 for M.W. Savage, the owner of the racehorse Dan Patch, and still has its 1880 depot, returned to town in 2006. Older homes and buildings there mean older pipe, and older pipe means we expect roots, scale and tight crawl spaces.

Newer homes sit along County Road 42 and Highway 13 toward the Minnesota River, near Savage Fen and Murphy-Hanrehan Park Reserve. Near the Credit River and Eagle Creek, basements see seasonal groundwater, and our sump pump repair work picks up in spring.
