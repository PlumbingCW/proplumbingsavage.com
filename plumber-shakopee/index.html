Problem one: sewer lines in a fast-growing city.

Shakopee grew from 20,568 people in 2000 to 43,698 in 2020, much of it after U.S. Highway 169 was realigned in 1996. Fast growth means many homes built within a few years of each other, on lots graded in similar ways. When settlement or builder-grade joints begin to fail, they fail in clusters.

A sewer line with a sag or an offset joint backs up repeatedly. Our camera inspection shows which. If the pipe is damaged, sewer line repair follows. If it is only full of grease or roots, sewer line cleaning is enough.

Problem three: water that leaves scale.

The 2025 Shakopee Public Utilities report does not state hardness in grains per gallon. Test your tap with a kit, or ask the utility what your address receives. The neighboring Burnsville and Prior Lake reports state 25 to 26 and 19.3 grains per gallon, and many homes in this region need a softener.

Scale is the reason a water heater repair often comes down to an element or a rumbling tank. A water softener reduces that scale.
