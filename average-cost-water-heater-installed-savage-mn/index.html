Average Cost of a Water Heater Installed in Savage, MN

The unit, the labor, the parts and the cleanup: how to read the whole bundle in an installed price.

By the Savage Pro Plumbing team. Published and updated 2026-11-10.

Talk to a plumber(952) 866-9120Call Now

When someone says a water heater costs a certain amount installed, they mean a bundle of items. The tank, the labor, the parts and the cleanup all matter. Two quotes that look different may include different things. This guide breaks the bundle apart so you can compare fairly.

We do not list dollar figures, because prices move with the market and the home. What you can do is learn the pieces and ask for each one.

Line item one: the unit.

The tank is the largest single item. Capacity, efficiency, brand and warranty all move the price. A forty-gallon standard tank is the baseline. A larger tank, a high-efficiency model or a heat pump water heater costs more.

Line item two: labor.

Labor depends on how hard the swap is. A like-for-like replacement in an open utility room is the easy case. A tight closet, a long vent run or a heavy tank on stairs takes more time.

Line item three: materials.

  • New water supply connectors or copper stubs.
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve and discharge pipe.
  • A drain pan, where there is room.
  • An expansion tank, where the system needs one.
  • Gas flex connector and sediment trap, for gas units.
  • Venting parts for gas units.
  • Electrical whip or breaker for electric units.

Some quotes bundle these items and some list them separately. Ask which items are included.

Code items and surprises.

When an old water heater is removed, hidden problems show up. A corroded shutoff valve, an undersized vent or a missing seismic strap, where required, can add work. A good installer points these out before work starts. Ask the City of Savage building department whether your job needs a permit.

Cheapest is not always lowest cost.

A low bid that skips an expansion tank or a drain pan can cost more in the end. Tanks fail eventually. A drain pan, a proper relief pipe and a labor warranty are the difference between a small leak and a flooded basement.

Warranties that matter and warranties that do not.

The tank warranty covers the steel tank against leaks for a stated number of years. It rarely covers labor to swap a failed unit, and it often excludes damage from hard water, a missing expansion tank or a bad install. Read the exclusions, not the headline number.

A labor warranty from the installer covers the work itself, such as a leaking connection. Ask how long it lasts and what voids it.

How to size the decision to your household.

  • Count peak simultaneous use: two showers, a dishwasher and a washer.
  • Check the first-hour rating, not just the gallon size.
  • Ask about recovery rate for large households.
  • Consider where the heater sits and how far it is from the farthest fixture.
  • Think about whether a recirculation loop is worth adding.

A tank that matches the home costs less to run and lasts longer than one that is too big or too small. Bring those answers to the quote conversation and the installer can sort options quickly.

Hidden costs that appear after a low bid.

A bid that leaves out the expansion tank, the drain pan, new shutoff valves or the vent fix is not cheaper, it is incomplete. When the installer arrives and finds a corroded valve, the price changes. Ask the bidder to list what would trigger a change order before you sign, and ask for the unit price of each likely extra.

Also ask who pulls any needed permit and who is present for any inspection. A job that skips a required inspection can cause trouble when you sell the house.

Keep every document. The invoice, the model and serial numbers and the warranty card belong in one folder, because a warranty claim without them is a hard conversation.

What to know in Savage.

The City of Savage buys about 71.5 percent of its distributed water from Burnsville, whose 2025 report states hardness of 25 to 26 grains per gallon. Savage's report does not list hardness, so test your tap. Hard water reduces tank life, which is one reason the warranty and anode rod matter.

Minnesota requires plumbing contractor licensing in communities over 5,000 people, and its Department of Labor and Industry offers a license lookup. Check your installer before you sign.

Source type: City water report and state agency page

Code and sources.

Minnesota Rules chapter 4714 is the state plumbing code and the standard a licensed plumber works to. The Department of Labor and Industry states that it incorporates the 2018 Uniform Plumbing Code with Minnesota amendments. Your city building department decides when a permit or inspection is needed, so check with them before work begins.
