A faucet that will not shut off, a valve that will not turn or a spray from under the sink is an emergency when you cannot stop the water. We answer 24/7 in Savage for these calls, and same-day service is available when we can. If you cannot close the fixture, find the shutoff valves under the sink or at the main.

If the shutoff valves under the sink also fail to close, shut the whole house off at the main. Then call us.

When a faucet or valve problem is urgent.

  • Water spraying from a supply line or under-sink valve.
  • A shutoff valve that will not close.
  • A tub spout or shower that runs constantly.
  • Water dripping behind the wall near a fixture.
  • A frozen or split pipe at an exterior hose bib.

Repair or replace the faucet?

A single drip is rarely worth replacing a good faucet. A corroded fixture with worn parts that are no longer made is another matter. We tell you which side you are on and give you a clear price for each.

Some faucet parts are discontinued. When a cartridge cannot be found, a new faucet is the cleaner answer. We can supply one or install yours.

Hard water and fixtures in Savage.

Mineral buildup from hard water, with Burnsville's supply reported at 25 to 26 grains per gallon, clogs aerators and stiffens cartridges. Cleaning an aerator takes minutes. Replacing a stiff cartridge brings a faucet back to life. Both are cheaper than a new faucet.

A water softener can reduce this wear throughout the house.

Which faucet do you have?

Faucets come in four basic designs, and the design decides the repair. Compression faucets have two handles that screw down onto a rubber washer. Ball faucets have a single handle on a rounded cap. Cartridge faucets have a single or double handle that slides or turns a plastic or brass cartridge. Ceramic disc faucets use a cylinder with two polished discs.

Compression washers wear and drip. Ball faucets leak at the base from worn seals. Cartridges stiffen or crack. Ceramic discs rarely wear but crack from grit. When you call, tell us how many handles and how they move. We bring the matching parts.

Photos help. Take one of the whole faucet and one under the sink showing the supply lines and shutoff valves. We can often tell the brand and the repair from a picture before the visit.

If a part is no longer made, we say so and quote a replacement faucet instead of promising a repair that cannot be done.
