A sump pump that fails during a heavy rain can put inches of water in a basement within hours. We answer 24/7 in Savage for sump pump emergencies, and same-day service is available when we can. If water is rising, move stored items up, switch off power to the basement only if the panel is dry and call us.

If the pump runs but the pit keeps filling, the pump may be undersized or the discharge may be blocked. If it does not run at all, the switch, the power or the motor has failed.

When a sump pump failure is an emergency.

  • The pit is full and the pump is silent.
  • The pump runs constantly and the water level does not drop.
  • A power outage during a storm with no battery backup.
  • Water on the basement floor around the pit.
  • A pump that cycles on and off every few seconds.

Repair, replace or add backup.

Most pumps last several years. A pump past that age that fails on a stormy night is cheaper to replace than to repair. A battery backup or a water-powered backup keeps the basement dry when the power fails, which is when storms often cause outages.

Savage water conditions.

Savage sits in the Minnesota River valley. The Credit River and Eagle Creek cross the city, and the Savage Fen wetland complex covers hundreds of acres within the refuge. Spring snowmelt and heavy summer storms can raise groundwater near these areas. Basements in lower areas see more sump activity than those on high ground.

In winter, the discharge line can freeze at its outlet. We route and slope it with this in mind, and we check the outlet when we service a pump in fall.

A fall checklist for your sump pit.

  • Pour a bucket of water in the pit and confirm the pump starts and empties it.
  • Clean any debris from the pit and the pump intake.
  • Check that the discharge line is clear and its outlet is not buried.
  • Confirm the check valve clicks shut after the pump stops.
  • Test the battery backup, if you have one.
